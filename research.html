Why one crop first?

Use the same garden-cress lot for both NY kit tests. This makes water-path and handling problems easier to interpret than a mix of plants with different germination rates. Compare like-sized seeded patches and similar light; different tray geometries mean this remains a practical pilot, not an isolated causal test.

In Japan, use repeated batches of the same fast crop for robot-assisted versus human-baseline care. Assign comparable positions, keep seed lot/medium/density/recipe consistent, log all interventions, and distinguish tray-level outcomes from individual-seed counts. The value of multiple waves is checking whether a result repeats—not collecting variety for its own sake. Add another species later only to test a specific generalization question.

Crop outcomes

Emergence timing, visible survival/coverage, photos at fixed positions, healthy seed leaves, moisture consistency and the actual harvest when available.

Robot outcomes

Valid sensing, correct target, successful/unknown delivery, spills, recoveries, human interventions and review time. A green crop alone does not prove autonomous care.

Use a manual care baseline. Do not withhold necessary water to preserve a comparison; intervene and record it. Six NY days can establish feasibility and expose failures, not prove a statistically reliable autonomy advantage.
