FIELD GUIDE / ROBOT ASSEMBLY

Assemble the robot, one working step at a time.

A numbered build guide for the recorded WowRobo two-wheel Combo, Mac, powered hub and farm accessories. Each step names the parts, connections and a completion check.

Build the robot first, then add one light tool. For the current paper-grown microgreens, our recommendation is to defer the moisture probe, probe relay and contact holder. Existing cameras plus human water checks support the first supervised trial. This does not yet provide unattended watering.

The current vendor listing calls this XLeRobot 0.4.0. Check the packing list and labels before assembly; the delivered revision has not been inspected here.

1–4 · Build a stable, dry robot

Step 01

Lay out and identify the parts

On the bench: WowRobo boxes, IKEA cart, hub, Mac; leave the battery disconnected.

  1. Group the two arms and their motor controllers, two drive assemblies, head assembly, three cameras, bracket/fastener bags, two motor supplies and supplied USB/motor leads. Keep the original bags with their labels.
  2. Photograph motor and adapter labels. Confirm this is the two-wheel servo version and that each motor supply matches its controller and motors. An adapter fitting the socket is not sufficient.
  3. Set aside the farm sensors, growing parts and battery. Have a screwdriver/hex keys matching the supplied screws and a borrowed multimeter for electrical checks.
Step 02

Assemble the IKEA cart and drive base

On the bench: Cart, two drive modules, printed brackets, bearings, supplied screws and motor extensions.

  1. Assemble the cart to its own manual. Before adding the arms, compare the bracket fit with the two-wheel vendor video; do not force a bracket designed for a different cart size.
  2. Build and mount each drive module in the video’s orientation and screw order. Use the kit’s bearings, wheel hubs and fasteners; do not substitute the older three-wheel plate.
  3. Route supplied motor extensions upward with protected edges and slack. Keep wheels free of dangling leads. Do not cut cart mesh until the delivered bracket and cable route establish where an opening is needed.
Step 03

Mount the arms and head

On the bench: Assembled follower arms, top mounts/clamps, head pan/tilt parts, three camera mounts.

  1. Support each arm while fastening its mount to the top structure in the vendor orientation. Skip rebuilding an already assembled arm; check for loose joints and cracked prints.
  2. Assemble the head pan/tilt mechanism using the supplied hardware. Mount the head camera and each wrist camera if not already fitted.
  3. Leave the grippers empty. Place the intended plant surface beside the parked cart and check approximate reach without forcing any joint. The planter table is separate from the robot cart.
Step 04

Finish robot cabling before power

On the bench: Supplied motor harnesses, camera leads, motor-controller USB cables and cable retainers.

  1. Use the delivered wiring map to connect each motor chain to the correct controller, including head and drive extensions. Label the two controller USB leads so they cannot be confused later.
  2. Keep motor power disconnected while changing motor plugs. Match connector orientation and pin order; do not splice the farm wire into the motor chain.
  3. Retain camera and motor cables at fixed points, with service loops at moving joints. Check that wrist motion cannot pull a plug or stretch a cable.

For unassembled arms, use the SO101 joint-by-joint instructions. Both XLeRobot arms are followers; a generic leader/follower tutorial does not make one of these a leader.

5–8 · Connect the Mac and prove basic motion

Step 05

Connect the powered hub and identify USB devices

On the bench: RSHTECH RSH-ST07, its adapter and upstream cable, MacBook, robot USB leads.

  1. Connect the hub’s own supplied power adapter, then its upstream data cable to the Mac’s USB 3-capable port. Keep the Mac on its charger. Do not assume a downstream charging port is the hub’s upstream port.
  2. Plug in one motor controller at a time and record its serial-device name, then connect each camera and identify head, left wrist and right wrist by covering the lens briefly.
  3. Use the connection checklist below for port labels. Nothing on a USB cable alone powers the arm motors.
Step 06

Install a matching robot software version

On the bench: Mac, XLeRobot source and compatible LeRobot environment.

  1. Follow the XLeRobot installation guide and its compatible LeRobot revision; record both versions. Do not mix commands from old tutorials with a newly upgraded environment.
  2. Use the SO101 guide’s port-discovery tool to map the actual Mac serial ports. Configure those paths in the robot settings instead of Linux /dev/ttyACM names.
  3. Select the two-wheel configuration and its expected motor map. Check that the installed code supports the delivered base before commanding it. macOS support for individual serial tools is not proof that every full-robot demo works.
Step 07

Power, identify and calibrate one arm at a time

On the bench: Matched kit motor supply, empty supported arm, reachable power disconnect.

  1. Confirm adapter input accepts the local mains and output matches the motor labels. Use the supplied wall supplies for commissioning. Apply motor power last; disconnect it before changing motor wiring.
  2. Check the preconfigured motor IDs before changing them. If configuration is required, use the documented one-motor-at-a-time procedure; never rename a whole connected chain blindly.
  3. Calibrate each follower arm with a unique name. Follow the tool’s midpoint and range prompts with torque disabled as directed; support the arm and stay within mechanical limits. Save the resulting calibration files.
Step 08

Test cameras and small movements without water

On the bench: Assembled robot, all USB devices, empty grippers and empty work area.

  1. Start a single-joint control example at conservative motion limits. Test direction and stopping on one arm, then the other, then the head. Keep base motion disabled during the first farm setup.
  2. Open the camera streams together at modest supported resolution/frame rate. Label the views and check for dropped frames while moving an arm; lower bandwidth if necessary.
  3. Test the configured stop action with empty grippers and supported arms. Once controls are dependable, rehearse reaching an empty tray and returning to rest.

References: XLeRobot installation, SO101 port setup/calibration, single-arm examples. These are setup instructions; no robot software has been installed or tested by this website.

9–11 · Add just one light sensor

Step 09

Wire a short light-sensor circuit on the bench

On the bench: One ELEGOO ESP32, one BH1750, USB data cable, breadboard and jumper wires.

  1. With USB unplugged, read the labels on both boards. Fit headers only if needed; solder loose headers into their plated holes. Already installed headers require no soldering.
  2. For a verified ESP-WROOM-32 board, our proposed assignment is BH1750 VCC to ESP32 3V3, GND to GND, SDA to GPIO21 and SCL to GPIO22. Match by GPIO label, not physical pin number. Confirm the actual breakout accepts 3.3V and its bus pull-ups do not exceed 3.3V.
  3. Check for shorts, then power the ESP32 only through its USB data cable from the hub. Configure firmware to the same SDA/SCL pins, detect the sensor and log lux over serial. Cover/uncover the sensor and confirm the reading changes.
Step 10

Fit the printed paddle and flexible harness

On the bench: Light paddle prototype, BH1750, flexible wire, retained connector and dry ESP32 enclosure.

  1. Dry-fit the actual board to the paddle; the prototype is not a guarantee of fit. Secure the board without covering the light-sensitive face or shorting its underside.
  2. Replace the short bench jumpers with a retained four-conductor harness. Keep the ESP32 on the cart and the light board on the paddle. The robot’s supplied servo cables are occupied and are not a spare light-sensor bus.
  3. Provide strain relief at the paddle and cart, service loops at joints, and a parked tool rest. Test the entire intended reach while logging sensor errors. If the longer I²C link fails, shorten/rework it before trusting readings.
Step 11

Finish durable connections and repeatable light measurements

On the bench: Perfboard/terminals if needed, insulation, fasteners and tool rest.

  1. If using perfboard, solder the headers/terminal pins and required interconnects on the dry bench. Check continuity against the tested circuit. Breadboard contacts are temporary, not a moving-arm connector.
  2. Insulate exposed splices with suitable sleeves or enclosed connectors; heat-shrink is only needed where there is an exposed joint. Fasten the electronics clear of water and keep the USB connector accessible.
  3. Teach a pose at canopy height with the face upward and the robot out of its light path. Stop moving, collect repeated fresh readings and record pose/time. Park the light tool before picking up a bottle. Lux readings do not directly measure plant-useful PPFD.

The GPIO assignment above is a project proposal for the recorded ESP-WROOM-32 board, not a verified photo of the delivered ELEGOO pinout. Espressif reference. The board’s own pin labels and documentation take precedence.

12–14 · Add trays and supervised watering

Step 12

Assemble and test both growing kits by hand

On the bench: Cress trough, holder and four insets; square outer tray and insert; paper and water.

  1. Follow the combined growing guide for fit, leak and paper-wick tests. Use the square tray’s printed cover when available, or a loose household cover for the trial.
  2. Leave an accessible refill opening and inspection spot. Check that paper wets across the seeded area, not just where it first touches water. The square kit needs an actual capillary connection; simply adding water below an elevated insert does not prove self-watering.
  3. Start on a stable household surface. Add the modular printed table only after its actual joints, stiffness and reach are checked. Use repeatable tray locations so a taught pour target does not move.
Step 13

Teach an empty bottle pickup and return

On the bench: Small bottle, suitable outlet, bottle rest, robot gripper and empty tray.

  1. Check grip width and retention with an empty bottle. The selected Nalgene container has a wide mouth: validate a fitted spout or use an existing bottle with a controlled outlet. The rendered spout is not a finished printable design.
  2. Place the bottle in a repeatable rest outside the crop. Teach pickup, approach to the refill opening, upright recovery and return while empty.
  3. Check wrist-camera view, clearance above the tray rim and cable slack through the whole path. A manual spray bottle is for hand use until trigger actuation is proven.
Step 14

Try tiny supervised pours and record results

On the bench: Small measured water fill, kitchen measuring container, household catch dish and towel.

  1. First pour into a kitchen measure over a catch dish, with a person supervising. Record starting fill, pose and actual water delivered. Repeat across different fill levels; tilt duration alone does not establish a dose.
  2. After that works, try a small refill of the empty planter. Check for overflow and verify the paper still receives water. Keep the Mac, hub and sensor electronics clear of the wet area.
  3. For the initial crop, a person checks water reserve and paper wetness and authorizes each refill. Cameras record evidence; an obscured view is unknown. Do not replace the removed probe with an unvalidated camera-only autonomous watering rule.

15–16 · Add only what the next test needs

Step 15

Keep optional sensor parts off the first build

On the bench: SHT31, moisture probe, relay modules and moisture-holder prototypes.

  1. An optional SHT31 measures air temperature/humidity. If wanted, mount it on the dry body in ventilated air, away from motor/electronics heat; it does not need to ride on the arm. Add it to the verified 3.3V-compatible I²C bus only after checking its labels and address.
  2. Defer the SparkFun moisture probe and its power relay for the paper-tray build. They are retained in the reference diagrams for a future comparison experiment, not required connections.
  3. If later testing the probe, validate repeated contact on sacrificial paper, switched power, dry/damp/wet discrimination and contamination/cleaning before mounting it near a crop. A water-depth solution is a different experiment.
Step 16

Commission battery power and mobility separately

On the bench: Owned Anker C300 DC, verified conversion cables/adapters, matched robot inputs.

  1. Keep using wall power until the motor power path is checked. The transcript mentions two USB-C PD-to-12V cables, but their actual availability and electrical suitability remain unverified.
  2. For every powered device, verify negotiated output voltage, polarity, barrel size, continuous/peak current and simultaneous-port behavior. The hub needs its own matched supply path; it cannot be assumed to run from an arbitrary USB-A port.
  3. Secure the battery low on the cart. Test power stability with the full load before untethered driving. Use the two-wheel base profile and a clear area for the first drive test; farm care remains parked.
