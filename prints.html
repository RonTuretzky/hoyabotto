Printer status

No planter part exists. The September 22 K1 Max jobs did not produce usable parts; that printer now clicks while feeding (nozzle clog). The earlier claims on this page that a tray had completed are withdrawn; nothing has been fit-, leak- or wick-tested. Printing continues on the M5C.

K1 Max unjam procedure (if it is used again)

  1. Clear the bed. Heat the nozzle to about 220–230 °C from the touchscreen.
  2. Push a 0.4 mm cleaning needle up into the nozzle tip from below and work it a few times.
  3. Extrude a short length by hand. A smooth steady line means the clog is cleared.
  4. If it still clicks, unload the filament and push the jam down from above with the long clog-poke rod while hot; failing that, swap the nozzle with the kit wrench while hot.

Suitable kits: K1 nozzle kit with 0.4 mm needles, spare nozzles and wrench or needle kit with clog-poke rod. Check the nozzle style against your unit before relying on the spares. Creality’s K1 Max clog procedure.

Nests, tag tiles, light paddle, bottle rest

The robot needs each tray in a known place. A printed nest plate (200 × 165 × 10 mm) locates the cress trough: a 181.5 × 86.5 mm pocket with three stepped ledges as a lead-in, a 6 mm lip, a 48 mm front apron carrying a 40.5 × 40.5 mm AprilTag recess and two M4 holes, two rear tabs, and a 30 mm notch on the short side so the bottle spout reaches the refill opening. Tag tiles are real AprilTag 36h11 id 1 and id 2 (40 × 40 × 1.6 mm, raised cells; make the raised cells black with a filament change at Z = 1.0 mm or a marker). The light paddle (150 × 24 × 8 mm) is a gripper handle with grooves, a BH1750 pocket and a cable channel; the bottle rest is Ø70 × 12 mm with a 56 mm bore and a 40° lead-in. All fit the M5C bed and print flat without supports.

Modular Table (100% printed): can the M5C print it, and how long?

Aj_Awesome1’s free CC0 design uses printed top tiles, rods and snap connectors. Yes, it fits the M5C: every part was designed for a Prusa MINI (180 × 180 bed), smaller than the M5C’s 220 × 220. Time: the creator’s pictured table is 63 h 08 m and 909 g of PLA on the MINI; the M5C is a faster machine, so expect roughly 40–45 h and about one spool for that size. A top wide enough for two nest plates (≈ 5 × 3 tiles) is closer to 55 h and 1.2 kg.

The 13 editable .3mf files are now downloaded (mirror, CC0) and every one fits the M5C: tiles 101.8–141.8 mm square × 12.6 mm thick (printed on edge in the original slices), rods 71 and 111 mm tall, connectors ≤ 63 mm. Slice them for the M5C at 0.2–0.25 mm. A two-nest top is 4 × 2 of the 80x80 tiles (≈ 57 × 28 cm). Recommendation: do not block on it. The robot only needs the nest plates at a repeatable height; any stable surface with the plates screwed down works on day one, and the table can print in the background over the first week. Height target: robot shoulders on the cart minus 5–10 cm.

File blocker: the received print_files.zip contains five pre-sliced Prusa MINI G-code files and a PDF. We still need ALL MODEL FILES (538 KB), the 13 editable 3MF models. Do not run the MINI files on the K1 Max.

Moisture probe holder

Optional experiment, deferred for the paper-tray build. Carrier and clamp prototypes exist. If revisited, add a depth stop referenced to the tray rim and gentle compliance before touching thin paper. Measure the delivered board, gripper and growing depth; don’t assume the rigid prototype is ready.

Light paddle

One grippable carrier holds one BH1750 face upward. Keep the sensing face clear of the arm/cable shadow; strain-relieve the moving cable. Tool is parked when watering.
