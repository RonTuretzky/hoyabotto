Kesennuma labor-saving application cases

Public excerpt from an anonymized English summary supplied to the Hoya Botto project on 9 October 2026. The source is Kesennuma_LaborSaving_Cases_EN.docx. This page reproduces selected reported values and limitations. It is a project-prepared excerpt, not a city publication or independent audit.

Application forecasts, not measured results. The source says some projects are still in progress and none has run for a full year. All savings, output and productivity gains below are planned values. The four cases are seafood-related applicants, not necessarily the four projects approved in March. They are not a representative sample of all manufacturing.

Amounts and percentages are rounded as in the supplied summary. Case A assumes 255 working days and Case C 250 days. In Case D, the three workers move to another product line; workers before and after the washer still help load and unload. The stated removal of labor from the washing step does not mean the whole line runs without people or that payroll falls.

Case C forecasts capacity gains but does not include them in its productivity calculation because additional sales are uncertain. Case D forecasts output rising from 1,680 kg to 2,100 kg/day (25%). The summary reports labor-productivity increases of 4% for A, 70% for B and 3.6% for D; C is not calculated.

How application results are defined

  • Reduction in work time per day on the production line that includes the process.
  • Increase in labor productivity, defined as gross profit per employee, with part-time hours converted into full-time equivalents.

The source says post-project verification and reporting procedures are still being checked. Hoya Botto proposes using these definitions when establishing trial baselines; no city endorsement is claimed.

This is the location of the supplier providing the quote, not the location of equipment manufacturing, installation work or profit retention. Some local suppliers have headquarters elsewhere and most machine manufacturers are outside the city or prefecture. The source warns that the value actually retained locally is probably much smaller. It provides no confirmed shared-machine or co-development savings examples.

Limits when using the cases

  • Annual maintenance costs and measured annual results are unavailable.
  • Released time can mean staff redeployment or more capacity, without cash expenditure falling.
  • The source's simple paybacks divide investment by forecast annual savings; they do not establish a full investment return after all costs.
  • Local project costs span about ¥4.9m–¥22m. These are different scopes, not quotations for a complete robot cell or full-factory automation.
  • The four cases do not validate the deck's manufacturing-wide adoption assumptions, ¥725m–¥1.79bn gross time-value scenarios, shared-engineering savings model or program net-savings target.
  • Companies, suppliers and machine manufacturers remain anonymous. No identities are inferred or linked to the company's logos shown elsewhere in the deck.
