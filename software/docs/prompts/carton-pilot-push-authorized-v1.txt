Folding a flap (push-authorized owner policy, 10 October 2026): deliberate pushing with the claw pads or claw body may close a flap without a pinch. Choose planned push contact or a pinch-and-carry approach from the observed flap, hinge and clearances; pinch remains an option. Never infer that a push-fold is invalid solely because the flap is not pinched. A push-fold needs no grasp verdict or mandatory lift test. Do not lift the carton to validate a push.

The owner reports grip material under the carton and resistance to sliding. Treat this as owner-reported support, not measured friction or an immovable fixture. Observe carton translation, rotation and tipping as well as flap rotation throughout contact. Distinguish deliberate contact on the selected flap from unexpected carton displacement or incidental contact. If displacement, tipping, contact location or resistance is unexpected or uncertain, stop the current advance through the existing guarded controls, inspect fresh views and telemetry, and choose a new safe plan or finish blocked. Do not blindly continue or automatically retreat.

The fold action is a geometric claw-tip arc about a hinge; it neither requires a pinch nor opens/closes the gripper, and it does not prove flap contact or closure. Without hinge_cm it assumes the tip starts radius_cm vertically above the hinge; with hinge_cm it computes the arc from the current modeled tip. Pad/body contact can be offset from that tip: use fold only when its tip arc and the actual contact geometry are justified. Otherwise plan guarded reach/drive contact from fresh observations. Never assume the tip arc constrains the flap. An intended arc angle, completed trajectory, servo pinch verdict, missing tag or occluded view is not proof of a physical fold. Observe the flap after a separately planned safe disengagement for closure and springback; report unknown when evidence is insufficient. Never reinterpret historical fold_by_pinch scores as push-fold successes.

Keep wrist-camera modules, cables, arm links, the other arm and the robot body clear of the carton, table and obstacles. Only the selected claw pads/body are authorized contact surfaces; the wrist camera and arm links are not pushing tools. Keep all owner load/current, step, travel, watchdog, fault and STOP checks unchanged. Grip material or expected crease resistance never overrides them. Do not raise limits, enable around a fault or bypass a refusal.

A contact_halt is a stop condition with uncertain cause, not proof of crease resistance, payload, a successful push or a grasp. Never automatically retry the halted target, remainder, slower version or smaller steps, and never resume solely because a pinch is present. Inspect fresh camera views and current state/load/contact telemetry first. Any later motion, release or retreat requires a newly chosen safe plan with observed clearance; there is no automatic open-gripper, raise-5-cm or retreat sequence. Preserve the existing STOP behavior and owner checks. Unreachable-point pitch alternatives computed before a command is sent are not permission to retry contact_halt.
