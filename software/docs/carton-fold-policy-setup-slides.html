Carton fold policy · station setup

Set up the box for the learned two-flap fold

A step-by-step guide to laying out the robot, table, carton and cameras so the simulation-trained policy sees what it was trained on, then to running it safely for the first time.

Before you start

Safety first, then gather the kit

Safety rules

  • Someone holds STOP (robot_stop) whenever a motor is enabled.
  • The 12 V switch is within reach. Switch it off before touching any servo wire.
  • Nobody inside the arms' sweep while they move.
  • First motion runs use no carton, free air, 10 steps.
  • Never relax limits in farm/safety/rules.py to make it work.

What you need

  • Tape measure (mm) and painter's tape
  • Phone with an inclinometer app
  • An empty carton, 379 × 283 × 108 mm, 140 mm flaps
  • A flat platform or table you can set to the right height
  • Four printed floor AprilTags for the camera pose: img/fold-policy-setup/fold-pose-tags-40-43.pdf
  • The 12 box tags: img/fold-policy-setup/fold-box-tags.pdf, plus scissors, a ruler and glue stick
  • The robot: both SO101 arms, head OAK-D Lite, both wrist cameras

Before step 1 · what the words mean

Arm base, pan axis, 220 mm, 120 mm

  • Arm base: the black block each arm stands on, with the shoulder motor inside. It is bolted to the robot and never moves.
  • Pan axis: the vertical line each arm swings left and right around.
  • 220 mm: from one pan axis to the other, centre to centre.
  • 120 mm: from the bottom of the arm bases straight down to the table top the carton stands on.

Animation: the XLeRobot model's cart, mast and head with the two SO-101 arms, table and carton exactly as the policy trained. Photo: your robot on 8 October. The pan-axis line in the photo is approximate.

Go / no-go

The policy was trained with the arm-base origins 220 mm apart, as in the robot model. The model turns its bases sideways, which would put the pan axes about 310 mm apart instead.

Whatever you measure, send it. If it isn't 220 mm, new demonstrations get recorded at your number before training: a few hours of compute, not days.

Step 2

Table top 120 mm below the arm bases

Owner15–30 min
  1. Measure from the table top straight up to the bottom of each arm base: where the black block sits on its mounting plate (blue line in the photo on the "words" slide).
  2. Raise or lower the platform (boards, books, a box) until both arms read 120 mm.

  4. If the base underside is hidden, measure floor → base and floor → platform, then subtract.
Your 70 cm table

Measure the floor → underside of each arm base and subtract 70 cm. You want 12 cm, i.e. bases at about 82 cm off the floor.

  • About 82 cm (as docs/carton.md says): the 70 cm table is right as it is.
  • About 77.5 cm (as the robot model says): the table is about 4.5 cm too high. Use a lower table (about 65.5 cm), or send the number and the demos get re-recorded at your height (untested below 12 cm; a few hours of compute).

Step 3

Square the cart, then tape the carton's spot

Owner15 min

Cart to table edge

  1. From each shoulder-pan axis, measure straight out to the near table edge.
  2. Move the cart until both sides read 111 mm. That is 150 mm from the base line; the pan axis sits 39 mm in front of it.
  3. If left and right differ, the cart is turned. Rotate it until they match.

Carton position

  1. Near wall of the carton 10 mm back from the table edge.
  2. Carton centre 10 mm to the robot's left of the midpoint between the arms (anywhere from 25 mm left to 5 mm right works).
  3. Square to the table edge, within 4°.
  4. Tape around the carton's footprint, so every run starts in the same place.

Owner5 min

Material is a guess

The simulation assumed a crease stiffness and friction, and trained across a range of them. Pre-folded, softer cartons can sag past flat. If you can, measure the crease with a luggage scale and send the reading.

Step 4b · box tags

Print the 12 box tags: the policy uses them

Owner10 min
  1. Print img/fold-policy-setup/fold-box-tags.pdf (2 pages) at 100% / actual size, never "fit to page".
  2. Measure a black square with a ruler. The wall and floor tags must be 45 mm and the flap tags 35 mm. If not, fix the print scale and print again.
  3. Cut on the dashed lines, keeping the white border. Every tag has its ID and an arrow: "up" points up on the box.
  4. Stick them flat with glue stick or clear tape over the white border only. Keep tape off the black squares and avoid shiny tape.
Why the tags matter

The simulated carton the policy learned on carries these 12 tags. On 4 unseen starts, the same policy folded 4/4 with the tags and 2/4 without them. Without tags, one run pushed the left flap outward and slid the carton 107 mm. Put them exactly where the next slides show.

Step 5 · automatic

The robot points its own head camera

AgentOwner approves5 min
  1. You: take the carton away, stand clear, keep STOP in reach. Raise both arms (the agent prints the pose: --print-arm-pose) so both gripper tags face the head.
  2. Agent: moves the head servos toward pan 0° and tilt 58° in small steps, and after every step measures where the camera really points from the two gripper tags. Wrong tick mappings don't matter: it measures, it doesn't trust the ticks. 
    python tools/auto_head_pose.py --pilot-root "$PILOT" --execute --operator <you> --out head-pose.json
  3. It writes head-pose.json: the camera's measured position and angle, the head ticks and the lens numbers (read from the OAK itself).
Today's limit

Your head can only tilt about 36° down (servo range ends at 2625 ticks; level is 2211). The policy trained at 58°. The tool stops at the limit and records the real angle; step 7 then re-films the training at that angle. Nothing is forced past the limit.

Step 10 · first motion
Know when it worked, and when to stop

Success looks like

  • The left claw braces the left short flap, the right claw folds the right short flap in, then the left one follows.
  • Both short flaps end flat (about 90°) and are held by the claws.
  • The carton barely moves (in simulation: about 1 mm, never more than 6 mm).
  • About 55 seconds from start to both flaps held.

Press STOP if

  • An arm presses down on the table, the cart or the other arm.
  • The carton slides more than a few centimetres or lifts.
  • A joint stalls or buzzes against a load.
  • Anything moves that you didn't expect.

Write what happened in software/STATUS.md, with the run directory.

Success and stop
One-page checklist

Setup checklist

  • STOP holder and 12 V switch ready
  • Arm spacing measured and photographed
  • Arm bases 120 mm above the carton surface, both sides within 3 mm
  • Cart squared: 111 mm from each pan axis to the table edge
  • Carton footprint taped: near wall 10 mm in, 10 mm left of centre
  • Empty carton, flaps up, short flaps facing the arms
  • Box tagged: 12 tags at the marked spots (45 / 35 mm checked), confirmed in a head-camera still
  • Head at pan 0°, tilt 58°, 4:3 stream; ticks noted
  • Wrist cameras see their claw tips
  • Head pointed and measured by the agent (auto_head_pose.py)
  • Wrist lenses calibrated from the box tags (capture_wrist_calibration.py)
  • Station check says OK (check_station.py)
  • Refit run and best checkpoint chosen (refit_fold_policy.py --launch)
  • Joint maps bound to the live calibration; arms moved to the start pose (move_to_start_pose.py)
  • Gripper streaming, link speed and joint speed confirmed
  • Dry run clean
  • Free-air 10-step run clean
  • Empty carton, one short flap, then both
