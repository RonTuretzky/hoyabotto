Set up the box for the learned two-flap fold

A step-by-step guide to laying out the robot, table, carton and cameras so the simulation-trained policy sees what it was trained on, then to running it safely for the first time.

Before you start

Safety first, then gather the kit

Safety rules

  • Someone holds STOP (robot_stop) whenever a motor is enabled.
  • The 12 V switch is within reach. Switch it off before touching any servo wire.
  • Nobody inside the arms' sweep while they move.
  • First motion runs use no carton, free air, 10 steps.
  • Never relax limits in farm/safety/rules.py to make it work.

What you need

  • Tape measure (mm) and painter's tape
  • Phone with an inclinometer app
  • An empty carton, 379 × 283 × 108 mm, 140 mm flaps
  • A flat platform or table you can set to the right height
  • Four printed AprilTags (tag36h11, 60–100 mm): tools/carton_tags_pdf.py
  • The robot: both SO101 arms, head OAK-D Lite, both wrist cameras

Step 1 · do this first

Measure the arm spacing

Owner5 min
  1. Find the shoulder-pan axis of each arm: the centre of the round plate that turns, above the base servo.
  2. Tape-measure between the two centres.
  3. Photograph the tape.
Go / no-go

The policy was trained with the arm-base origins 220 mm apart, as in the robot model. The model turns its bases sideways, which would put the pan axes about 310 mm apart instead.

Whatever you measure, send it. If it isn't 220 mm, new demonstrations get recorded at your number before training: a few hours of compute, not days.

Step 2

Set the carton surface 120 mm below the arm bases

Owner15–30 min
  1. Measure from the platform top up to the underside of each SO101 base.
  2. Raise or lower the platform (boards, books, a box) until both arms read 120 mm.
  3. Left and right must agree within 3 mm. If not, the cart or platform is tilted: shim it.
  4. If the base underside is hidden, measure floor → base and floor → platform, then subtract.
Your 70 cm table

Measure the floor → underside of each arm base and subtract 70 cm. You want 12 cm, i.e. bases at about 82 cm off the floor.

  • About 82 cm (as docs/carton.md says): the 70 cm table is right as it is.
  • About 77.5 cm (as the robot model says): the table is about 4.5 cm too high. Use a lower table (about 65.5 cm), or send the number and the demos get re-recorded at your height (untested below 12 cm; a few hours of compute).
Step 2 · height
Step 3

Square the cart, then tape the carton's spot

Owner15 min

Cart to table edge

  1. From each shoulder-pan axis, measure straight out to the near table edge.
  2. Move the cart until both sides read 111 mm. That is 150 mm from the base line; the pan axis sits 39 mm in front of it.
  3. If left and right differ, the cart is turned. Rotate it until they match.

Carton position

  1. Near wall of the carton 10 mm back from the table edge.
  2. Carton centre 10 mm to the robot's left of the midpoint between the arms (anywhere from 25 mm left to 5 mm right works).
  3. Square to the table edge, within 4°.
  4. Tape around the carton's footprint, so every run starts in the same place.
Step 3 · setback and carton spot
Step 4

Prepare the carton

Owner5 min
  1. Use an empty carton. Check it is 379 × 283 × 108 mm with 140 mm flaps.
  2. Stand all four flaps straight up, open.
  3. Turn it so the short flaps face the arms: one on the robot's left, one on its right. The long flaps are far and near.
  4. Place it in the taped footprint.
Material is a guess

The simulation assumed a crease stiffness and friction, and trained across a range of them. Pre-folded, softer cartons can sag past flat. If you can, measure the crease with a luggage scale and send the reading.

Point the cameras the way the policy expects

Wrist cameras = "left_wrist", "right_wrist"

  • Mounted as in the robot model, looking along the jaws.

Step 5 · cameras
Step 6a · camera poses

Print the tags and lay them out

Owner15 min
  1. Print fold-pose-tags-40-43.pdf at 100% / actual size (no "fit to page").
  2. Measure one tag's black square with a ruler. It should be 80 mm; write down what you get.
  3. Cut the four tags out, leaving the white border.
  4. Before the carton goes down, tape them flat on the platform, printed text upright, "TOP EDGE" pointing away from the robot:
    TagSidewaysOut from the base line
    4012 cm left25 cm
    4112 cm right25 cm
    4212 cm left40 cm
    4312 cm right40 cm

How to measure a tag spot

  • Sideways: from the midpoint between the two shoulder-pan axes. Robot's left is negative.
  • Out: from the line through the two shoulder-pan axes, toward the table, then add 39 mm. The base line sits 39 mm behind the pan axes.
  • Measure to the centre of each tag. Write the four actual spots down; exact placement doesn't matter, knowing it does.
  • Height: the tags lie on the carton surface, which is 120 mm below the bases after step 2.

Step 6b · camera poses

Set the head, read its lens, take a still

Owner15 min
  1. With STOP in reach, set the head to pan 0°, tilt 58° down with the robot's head controls. Check with the phone inclinometer on the camera body: 58° below horizontal.
  2. Read the head pan/tilt encoder ticks (read-only) and write them down.
  3. On the robot Mac, in Terminal (camera permission), read the OAK's factory lens calibration. This only reads; it moves nothing: 
    cd software
python tools/oak_intrinsics.py --width 640 --height 480 --out oak-640x480.json
  4. Save one still from the head OAK at 640×480, with all four tags visible: front.png.
Why 4:3

The policy saw a 4:3 image with a 54° vertical view. The usual 640×360 stream is cropped and too narrow.

Step 6c · camera poses

Compute the head camera's pose

Owner or agent5 min

Run this with your measured tag size and spots, in metres. Sideways: left negative. Out: as in 6a. Height: −0.120.

python tools/camera_pose_from_tag.py --image front.png --camera front \
  --intrinsics oak-640x480.json --tag-size 0.080 \
  --tag 40 -0.12 0.25 -0.12  --tag 41 0.12 0.25 -0.12 \
  --tag 42 -0.12 0.40 -0.12  --tag 43 0.12 0.40 -0.12 \
  --update station.json

This writes the camera's position and angle into station.json. Start from profiles/fold-station-xlerobot-220.json, set "measured": true, and fill in your step 1–3 numbers. Or just send me front.png, oak-640x480.json and your numbers, and I'll run it.

Check the answer

  • Lens about 42 cm above the bases and about 5 cm forward.
  • Pitch about 58° down.
  • If it's far off, a tag was probably turned the wrong way or a spot was mis-measured.

Step 6d · wrist cameras

Calibrate each wrist camera's lens

Owner15 min per camera
  1. Print fold-checkerboard-9x6-25mm.pdf at actual size. Measure one square (should be 25 mm). Tape it flat to a board.
  2. From each wrist camera, at the resolution the policy will use, save 15–20 photos of the board: near, far, tilted, in the corners. The whole board must be in every photo.
  3. Run, for each camera: 
    python tools/calibrate_camera_checkerboard.py \
  --images 'left_wrist/*.png' --inner 9 6 --square-mm 25 \
  --out left_wrist-640x480.json
  4. Send the two JSON files.

What it tells us

  • The field of view. The simulation assumed 90° vertical. If yours differs, the demos are re-filmed with your lens and the policy retrained (no re-recording).
  • Where a wrist camera sits is fixed by its mount on the gripper, so it needs no tags; just confirm it's mounted as in the robot model.
Step 6d · wrist cameras
Step 7

Send the measurements; the policy is refitted

Agent1–3 h compute

Layout matched (steps 1–4)

Only the cameras changed. The existing demonstrations are re-filmed from the measured camera positions (about 1 minute). Then the dataset is converted, the policy retrained on the cloud GPU (~30–60 min) and re-tested.

Layout differs

Spacing, height or setback can't be matched. New demonstrations are recorded at your measured layout (~20–40 min), then converted and trained the same way.

What you send

  • station.json (measured: true)
  • Photos of each tape measurement
  • One still per camera
  • Head pan/tilt ticks
Step 7 · refit
Step 8a · joint maps

Arm maps are accepted; bind them on the robot Mac

Owner + agentNothing moves5 min

The maps are in profiles/fold-joint-maps/. They use the digital twin's mapping: each arm joint's zero is the middle of its saved range (tick 2047 for every arm joint today), sign +1. The jaw is closed at the bottom of its range. You accepted this on 8 October; the files say "accepted, not measured".

On the robot Mac, tie them to the live calibration file:

cd software
python tools/bind_fold_joint_maps.py \
  --calibration <live saved calibration .json> --out bound-maps/

It refuses if any arm motor's homing offset or range changed since 8 October, meaning the arms were recalibrated. Then the maps get regenerated from a fresh read.

The head is the exception

The twin's head angles were off by about 15° tilt and 17° pan in the live check. Don't use them. The head camera pose comes from the tags in step 6.

Step 9

Dry run: the policy thinks, the arms don't move

Owner + agentNothing is sent

Arms released, supported at the training start pose (8b), no carton. No --execute, so no command reaches the motors.

python -m carton.fold_policy_runner --checkpoint <ckpt> \
  --transport api --pilot-root "$PILOT" \
  --joint-map left=bound-maps/left-joint-map.json --joint-map right=bound-maps/right-joint-map.json \
  --camera front=<head OAK> --camera left_wrist=<cam> --camera right_wrist=<cam> \
  --out <run dir> --max-steps 100

Check in the log

  • Joint angles match what you measure by hand.
  • No "start outside training" warning. Pose the arms by hand at the training start pose until it clears.
  • Proposed moves are small and sensible.
  • Speed: the effective rate is close to 10 per second.

Step 10

First motion, then the carton

Owner holds STOP

A · Free air

  1. Enable each arm (it holds in place).
  2. Add --execute --operator <your name> --max-steps 10.
  3. No carton, claws open, nothing in reach.
  4. The run ends holding. Support the arms, then robot_stop.

B · Longer free-air runs

Raise --max-steps only after reading ticks.jsonl from the last run: clamps, warnings, timing.

C · Empty carton

Only after every gap in step 8 is closed and the policy was refitted for your measurements. Start with enough steps for one short flap, STOP in hand, then both flaps.

Step 10 · first motion
Know when it worked, and when to stop

Success looks like

  • The left claw braces the left short flap, the right claw folds the right short flap in, then the left one follows.
  • Both short flaps end flat (about 90°) and are held by the claws.
  • The carton barely moves (in simulation: about 1 mm, never more than 6 mm).
  • About 55 seconds from start to both flaps held.

Press STOP if

  • An arm presses down on the table, the cart or the other arm.
  • The carton slides more than a few centimetres or lifts.
  • A joint stalls or buzzes against a load.
  • Anything moves that you didn't expect.

Write what happened in software/STATUS.md, with the run directory.
