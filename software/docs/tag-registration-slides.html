Overview
Current state · 7 October 2026

The registration code is ready and tested. It has never run on the robot.

Exists and tested (software)

  • Detection on the chat Mac: robot_get_tags (TagRobot). It only accepts hamming 0, margin ≥ 30 and edge ≥ 24 px, and rejects frames older than 2 s.
  • Metric tag pose (OpenCV IPPE) uses the frame's own intrinsics. The OAK's raw 640×360 stream carries factory intrinsics and distortion, so this works today.
  • Automatic registration robot_calibrate_tags(mode="registration") uses 8 fit poses, 3 held-out poses and the OpenCV hand-eye solver. It writes tag-registration.json only when the fit passes.
  • robot_get_registered_tags re-checks the binding and the gripper-tag agreement on every read.
  • Tests: 178 passed today (tag, adapter, geometry, sampling, registration; no hardware). The rendered end-to-end simulation passed on 6 Oct.

Blocked today, and why

  • Never run: there is no tag-calibration-runs/ folder on the chat Mac and zero accepted right-arm poses.
  • No right-arm config in the robot API: gemma_reach_planner.CONFIGS only knows left. Registration refuses before it enables motors, because it needs robot_get_arm_pose(right) to report a feetech_degrees_v1 config.
  • Zero/sign: ranges and homing offsets exist, but no measured model_zero_tick/model_sign. The range-midpoint mapping is only a candidate.
  • Tag sizes 60/40/40 mm are print-kit defaults. Nobody has measured them with a ruler.
  • Calibration mover (GemmaTransport) was written for the 6 Oct owner. It has not been run against today's paddle-success-v1 owner over the relay.

Not built

  • Grasp geometry: tag 3 → handle, jaw contact point (gripper_from_tool), workspace bounds.
  • From pose to motion: robot_plan_reach returns proposals only, and the repo says not to send them to the owner. Nothing turns a registered paddle pose into a move.
  • Wider calibration: the grid moves only 2 joints by ±96 ticks, and there is no joint-offset fit.

The 7 Oct paddle pickup succeeded without tags. It used encoder targets plus camera checks. That procedure stays valid. Qwen should not call robot_get_registered_tags as a prerequisite for it (TAG-TOOL-FAILURE-HANDOFF.md).

Step 1 of 15 · physical set-up

Measure and mount the three tags

Owner at robot30-45 minManual, no code

Why it matters

The pose distance scales with the black-square width you configure. A 41 mm print configured as 40 mm puts the tag 2.5 % too far away, about 15 mm at 600 mm. The hand-eye fit assumes tag 2 is rigid on the fixed gripper housing, so any flex or wobble shows up as fit error.

Actions

  1. Measure each black square (not the white border) in both directions to 0.5 mm. Expected: tag 1 = 60 mm, tags 2 and 3 = 40 mm.
  2. Check that each tag is flat: paper on a rigid backing, no curl, no bubbles, no glossy tape over the pattern, full white border.
  3. Tag 2 goes on the right gripper's fixed housing, not the moving jaw. Press it; it must not flex. It should face the head camera across the arm's working poses. (The housing has only a narrow flat band. docs/carton-apriltags.md proposes a 52 mm backing plate.)
  4. Tag 3 goes rigidly on the paddle, away from the handle's grip area. Take a photo with a ruler in frame.
  5. Tag 1 is taped flat on the table where it will stay, fully visible to the OAK.
  6. Send the numbers and photos to the agent. It writes them into apriltag-geometry.json with "source": "ruler, <date>, owner".

Done when

  • Three measured widths are recorded, with the method used.
  • All tags are flat and rigid, and there is a photo of each mount.
  • Only one copy of each ID is in the camera's view.

Risks

  • Re-mounting or bumping tag 2 later invalidates the registration (redo steps 10-11).
  • If tag 2 is hidden by the jaw or wrist camera in some poses, registration and every later registered read refuse (tag 2 must be visible in every frame).

Check that the camera measures distances correctly

Owner + agent20-30 minManual comparison

Why it matters

The factory intrinsics are scaled to 640×360 and have never been checked on this unit. At this resolution the code's own noise estimate is coarse. In the 7 Oct evidence, table tag z was ±6.4 mm at 634 mm, tag 2 was ±2.7 mm at 340 mm, and all three tags were flagged coarse_position_only. The rendered test missed its 5 mm target at 640 px and only passed at 1920×1440.

Actions

  1. Hold a spare tag of known size (or the paddle) square to the camera at three tape-measured distances, about 300, 450 and 600 mm. Call robot_get_tags each time.
  2. Compare the differences between the readings' center_camera_mm z values with the tape differences. This avoids needing the exact optical centre.
  3. With the paddle on the table, measure tag 1 centre to tag 3 centre with a ruler. Compare it with the distance between their center_camera_mm values.

Needs building if this fails

A higher-resolution OAK stream: the repo's farm/oak_camera.py hardcodes 640×360, and the live publisher is outside the repo. Or a camera's own intrinsic calibration (checkerboard), which is not in the repo. Either change means re-registering.

Done when (suggested, not in code)

  • Distance differences agree within 2 %.
  • The tag 1 ↔ tag 3 distance is within 5 mm of the ruler.
  • The results are recorded in STATUS.md.

Risks

  • Wrong tag widths from step 1 show up here as a constant scale error.
  • Accepting coarse numbers here sets a floor on the final reach error (step 14). Plan a camera-guided final approach either way.

Step 6 of 15 · arm model

Sanity-check joint zeros and directions

Owner at robot + agent30 minHelper needs building

Why it matters

The candidate mapping assumes that, for each joint, 0° is the midpoint of the saved range and the direction is +1. The ranges came from end-stop sweeps, so a midpoint need not be the URDF zero; the repo labels it "candidate pending physical fit". A sign flip or a large offset makes FK wrong away from the calibration poses, and a 2-joint fit can still pass locally.

Actions

  1. Direction: for each of the 5 positioning joints, the agent moves +40 ticks with robot_move_joint_targets {"arm":"right", ...} and the owner (or a camera) reports which way the link moved. The agent compares that with the motion the model predicts for +degrees.
  2. Zero: the agent reports the candidate joint angles for the current encoders. The owner checks the upper arm, forearm and wrist angles with a protractor or a phone inclinometer.
  3. Build: a short read-only script that prints the candidate degrees and the predicted link directions, reusing tag_registration.candidate_degrees and LeRobotSO101.forward.

Done when (suggested)

  • All 5 directions match.
  • Each joint is within about 5° of the predicted angle.

If a joint is off

Record measured model_zero_tick/model_sign (templates: python -m carton.servo kinematics-template, python -m farm.kinematics.analytical_reference template). Registration then needs a mapping other than feetech_degrees_v1, because assemble_dataset accepts only that one. That would need to be built.

Risks

  • Eyeballing angles is good to about ±3-5°, so this only catches gross errors. Fine accuracy comes from step 11.
  • Every move is real motion: keep steps short and watch them, with STOP in reach.

Run the registration

Owner supervisingAgent or Qwen triggers5-10 minExists, never run

What happens

The run takes a baseline observation, checks the right-arm config and FK, then enables the 5 right positioning joints (never the jaw, head, left arm or wheels). It visits 8 fit poses and 3 held-out poses in 16-tick observed steps, returns, releases (verifying torque 0 on all 16 motors) and fits offline: B_G · G_T = B_C · C_T with OpenCV Park, Horaud or Daniilidis, taking the best.

Trigger (explicit request only)

# in the chat
robot_calibrate_tags {"mode":"registration"}
# or from the chat Mac
cd "$SW" && PYTHONPATH=. "$PY" tools/calibrate_gemma_tags.py registration \
  --pilot-root "$PILOT" --execute

If rejected

No file is written and there is no automatic retry. Read failure.json or the residuals. Usual causes: tag 2 lost at an edge pose, too little rotation, a wrong joint mapping (step 6), or freshness/clock refusals (step 7).

Done when (enforced in code)

  • status: REGISTRATION_VALIDATED.
  • Train and held-out sets both have position RMS ≤ 2 mm, max ≤ 4 mm and orientation max ≤ 2°, with ≥ 8 and ≥ 3 poses.
  • Held-out poses differ from fit poses by ≥ 10 mm or 5°.
  • $PILOT/.private/tag-registration.json is written, with evidence in $PILOT/tag-calibration-runs/<ns>-registration/.
  • Fresh readback shows all 16 motors released.

Risks

  • STOP mid-run releases the arm over 2 s, so it may sag.
  • The result says motion_ready: false. A pass permits coordinate estimates, not grasps.

  1. Put the paddle at a new spot, not the 7 Oct one. Ask Qwen: "Get the paddle target and move to the pregrasp 60 mm away. Stop there."
  2. Measure the actual jaw-centre to handle offset with a ruler or a side photo.
  3. If it is close enough, continue with the existing paddle-success-v1 steps: align, close until resistance, small lift, hold, place, open, withdraw, release.
  4. Record in software/STATUS.md: run folders, residuals, the measured pregrasp error and the final release state. Push.

Done when (suggested)

  • Pregrasp error is ≤ 10 mm with no contact.
  • After final alignment, the handle is inside the open jaw.
  • Lift and hold are observed independently by the camera, and the release is verified.

Risks

  • Don't proceed if any registered read refused during the trial.
  • A jaw that stops short means possible contact, not a held paddle.
